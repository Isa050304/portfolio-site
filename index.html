Why the thread?

The red thread keeps showing up in my work for a reason.

It started as a visual detail and became a useful way to describe how ideas connect across a project.

The thread represents the way one decision can lead to another, even when the process is not perfectly linear. It can move, loop and change direction while still holding the work together.

I want the final work to feel resolved without hiding the thinking behind it. Research, testing and small visual decisions are part of the result, not separate from it.

How I work

I usually find the answer by making, changing and trying again.

My process is flexible, but these three stages appear in most projects. I move between them as the work develops rather than treating them as a fixed sequence.

01

Pull

I start by understanding what I am trying to say, who it is for and which questions are actually worth following.

02

Knot

I test directions, compare options and use the strongest ideas to shape a more focused visual system.

03

Resolve

I refine the hierarchy, remove what is unnecessary and keep the decisions that make the idea clearer.

A few things I care about

I want the work to feel clear, but never generic.

Concept first

I want visual choices to support the purpose of the project, not exist only as decoration.

Details matter

Type, spacing, rhythm and interaction shape how a project is understood, so I treat them as part of the concept from the beginning.

Room to play

A system can be functional and still have personality, contrast and a clear point of view.
